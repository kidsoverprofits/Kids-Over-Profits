Forbes, "Too-tough love?", March 22, 1999.
- WWASP, "a LaVerkin, Utah-based group," loosely directs seven schools in the U.S., Jamaica, Mexico and Western Samoa, marketed as Teen Help; about 950 teens enrolled; up to $54,000 a year; estimated $30 million-plus revenue.
- "Karr Farnsworth, president of WWASP" (in 1999); stays 12 to 18 months.
- "Robert [Lichfield] founded Cross Creek Manor in 1988, the oldest school in the WWASP network, established Paradise Cove and ran Teen Help before his retirement a few years ago."
- J. Ralph Atkin (SkyWest founder): attorney for Teen Help, registered agent for R&B Billing and Dixie Contract; Utah Commerce lists him as a WWASP trustee (he denies); co-owned Morava Academy.
- Morava Academy (Czech Republic) closed Nov. 1998 after Czech police charged its U.S. managers with child torture; Sunrise Beach (Mexico) shut May 1996.
- Brightway Adolescent Hospital (St. George) closed March 1998 amid a Utah Department of Health investigation.
- Attorney Thomas Burton had filed two federal cases against WWASP schools.
