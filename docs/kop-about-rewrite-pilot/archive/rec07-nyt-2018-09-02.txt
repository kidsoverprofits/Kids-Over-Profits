The New York Times, "'It's Like, Who's Next?': A Troubled School's Alarming Death Rate," Sept. 2, 2018.
- Four former students died within months of one another in 2015; at least seven more the next year. Alumni kept count: at least 87 by October [2017/18], then 88, 89, "No. 90, in Minnesota."
- "The school closed in 2014 after a drop in enrollment that followed a self-described truth campaign by alumni telling of abuses there: solitary confinement, so-called 'blackouts' of silence and isolation from others, the restraining of unruly [students]..."
- Emmanuel Argiros, "the son of the school's founders and its former president," declined to comment on the school's history.
- The count is led by Elizabeth Ianelli, 39, an alumna and former police instructor; username Survivor993 "for the number of days she spent at the school"; Facebook page ISeeYouSurvivor; lives in Carmel, N.Y.
- Family Foundation School, a small boarding academy in rural Hancock, N.Y., "Since its opening in the 1980s."
- Supports: the 2014 closure, the truth campaign, the count of 90, Ianelli/Survivor993. Does NOT give Ianelli's years at the school or a founding year beyond "the 1980s."
