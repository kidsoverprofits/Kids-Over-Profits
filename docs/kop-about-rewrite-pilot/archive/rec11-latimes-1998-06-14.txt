Los Angeles Times, Julie Cart, "A Puzzling Death at Boys Ranch," June 14, 1998 (Oracle, Ariz.).
- Nicholaus Contreraz, 16, of Sacramento, died March 2, 1998 at Arizona Boys Ranch's Oracle campus; Pima County Medical Examiner: empyema (2 1/2 quarts of pus in chest lining), plus strep and staph infections, pneumonia, chronic bronchitis; 71 cuts and bruises. Details from a 1,000-page Pinal County Sheriff's report.
- On Yellow Shirt status: made to sleep in soiled underwear, eat dinner on the toilet, carry a basket of soiled clothes and vomit; pushed in a wheelbarrow after collapsing; nurse Linda Babb repeatedly cleared him for exercise; camp physician Dr. Virginia Rutz was on probation with the osteopathic board.
- "nearly 100 child abuse complaints have been lodged against the ranch or its employees in the last five years--nine since Nick died in March. Twenty-one of the abuse claims have been substantiated by state officials."
- 1994: a Mississippi youth drowned in the Arizona Canal fleeing ranch employees (ruled accidental); afterward Mississippi froze placements, later resumed.
- "In 1995, Alameda County, Calif., withdrew 67 boys after half of them claimed abuse at the hands of ranch staff. The Alameda County Juvenile Court concluded there was 'systematic abuse.'"
- "In 1995, the ranch fired two employees who struck a 15-year-old California boy 25 to 30 times."
- 1996 internal DES memo: five employees complained the ranch "continues to abuse children, thwart regulations and use their political influence to combat noncompliance of licensing rules"; DES agreed to give the ranch 48 hours' notice before inspections.
- License put on provisional status for abuse three times; renewed in 1996 with stipulations on reporting and restraint training.
- 1987: Carl Prange (later Oracle director) suspended after 11 boys alleged choking, kicking, punching; criminal investigation dropped.
- Ranch receives $1.45 million a month from California; about 400 of ~500 youths from California; 1998 budget $26 million; seven campuses.
