The Salt Lake Tribune, Brooke Adams, "Founder's Passion Is Helping Youths," May 25, 2003 (republished by Inside Costa Rica).
- WWASPS (World Wide Association of Specialty Programs and Schools), St. George, president Ken Kay; "Founded in 1998 as a nonprofit organization."
- Roots in Provo Canyon School; "Robert Lichfield had been director of residential living at Provo Canyon ... He moved to La Verkin ... and with partner Brent Facer opened a new school in 1988 -- Cross Creek Manor, this time focusing on girls." Lichfield later created Teen Help to market it; the school later added a boys' program.
- Lichfield is a director of World Wide along with Kay and Facer.
- "Karr Farnsworth, who runs the three Cross Creek programs in LaVerkin, worked with Bob Lichfield at Provo Canyon School"; his wife and three daughters work at Cross Creek.
- Cross Creek is "the system's only treatment center"; tuition up to "$3,990 a month at Cross Creek"; $2,000 processing fee; average stay 16 months.
- No TV/internet; phone controlled, mail monitored; six levels; consequences include essays of 3,000-12,000 words; isolation rooms (Farnsworth "claims its isolation rooms are rarely used").
- "Some students and parents say they have been abused in World Wide schools -- forced to kneel for hours on concrete, physically restrained by staff and made to perform menial labor."
- Attorney Thomas Burton had brought seven suits against World Wide and its programs (three pending, four dismissed).
RESOLVES the co-founder conflict: the Tribune names Brent Facer as Lichfield's partner in opening Cross Creek (1988); Farnsworth ran it by 2003.
