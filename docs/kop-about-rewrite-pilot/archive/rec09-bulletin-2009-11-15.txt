The Bulletin (Bend), Keith Chu, "Ever unconventional, long controversial," Nov. 15, 2009.
- "An era ended when Mount Bachelor Academy closed Nov. 3, following allegations of child abuse by the state Department of Human Services." Located 26 miles east of Prineville.
- Methods originated in the Synanon self-help group; MBA "one of the last schools founded on a therapy for troubled teens that originated at ... CEDU" (consultants Woodbury, Jackson, Bodin, Croke). CEDU founder Mel Wasserman drew from Synanon; Wasserman died in 2002.
- DHS complaint found methods "punitive, humiliating, degrading and traumatizing," including "sexualized role play in front of staff and peers, requiring students to say derogatory phrases about themselves ..., requiring students to re-enact past physical abuse ..., permitting staff to engage in the usage of derogatory names, phrases and ridicule of students and deprivation of sleep."
- "In 1987, College Health Enterprises, a group of Southern California hospitals, decided to start an emotional-growth boarding school"; founder Linda Houghton; moved from Powell Butte to the former Mark's Creek Lodge, Ochoco National Forest.
- "In 1997 or 1998, College Health Enterprises spun off Mount Bachelor Academy and a handful of other youth facilities into the company that became Aspen Education Group." In 2006 CRC Health (an arm of Bain Capital) purchased Aspen.
- Utah: three children died in Aspen-owned Utah facilities since 2004 (two suicides; 2007 Brendan Blum, 14, bowel obstruction, at Youth Care of Utah, after counselors failed to call for medical help). Sergey Blashchishen, 16, collapsed and died on a SageWalk hike in Lake County in August 2009; DHS ordered SageWalk temporarily closed in September.
- Tuition $6,400 a month. CRC lost $141 million in 2008 (SEC filings).
- "In April, Oregon DHS opened an investigation ... On Nov. 3, the agency found nine confirmed allegations of abuse and ordered the school's license temporarily suspended." Executive Director Sharon Bitz initially said the school would appeal; CRC then filed a mass layoff notice.
- 10 former students interviewed earlier in 2009 described one or more of the practices in the state's complaint; manual labor "a week or even a month" as punishment. Consultant Alice Jackson stopped referring nearly 10 years earlier.
