Rewrite

The Academy at Swift River was a coed therapeutic boarding school for teenagers in Cummington, Massachusetts.[1][2][3] It opened in 1997 and was run by Aspen Education Group until it closed in 2013.[1][2][3] Its founding headmaster and much of its first staff came from CEDU in California and Hidden Lake Academy in Georgia, both emotional-growth schools.[4] Its sister school in Oregon, Mount Bachelor Academy, which used methods that began at CEDU, was shut down by the state in 2009 over sleep deprivation, aggressive confrontation groups and role-play.[5][6]

The school's 1998 staff list named John T. Powers as director and Rudolph Bentz as headmaster.[4] Bentz had spent 13 years at CEDU in Running Springs, California, and four as headmaster of Hidden Lake Academy in Dahlonega, Georgia, and most of the student-life counselors listed prior work at Hidden Lake.[4] Bentz was still headmaster in 2001.[7] The school joined the National Association of Therapeutic Schools and Programs in 1999.[8]

By the school's own description in 2008, it had about 100 students aged 14 to 17½, 70 percent of them boys, on a 636-acre campus, and they stayed an average of 15 months.[1] New students began in an orientation phase called "Pathways" before starting classes, and the school offered psychiatric "medication consultation and management".[1] By 2013 it listed approval from the Massachusetts Department of Education.[8] Aspen was then a division of CRC Health Group, which the private equity firm Bain Capital owned.[9]

The school's family handbook from about 2006 shows how closely it controlled students.[10] Counselors read and approved all mail going in and out, and new students could write only to parents, siblings and grandparents, earning the right to write to other relatives and then to approved friends in stages.[10] Staff put students "on bans" with one another, which forbade any communication between them, including eye contact.[10] The harshest consequence, a "Self Study" that usually lasted 10 to 12 days, meant losing all free time, doing work projects and early wake-ups every weekend, and "many bans."[10] Students went through five "Lifesteps" workshops, the name Mount Bachelor used for its own seminars, and took part in sweat lodges that staff ran.[10][11] The handbook described the school as "approved as a private boarding school" by the Massachusetts Department of Education.[10] In its 2009 NATSAP directory listing it reported 107 students and stays of 12 to 20 months, and gave its approval through the Mohawk Trail Regional School System and accreditation by the Commission on International and Trans-Regional Accreditation.[12] By its 2012–13 listing it had 75 students, stays of 12 to 15 months, a license from the Massachusetts Department of Early Education and Care, and accreditation from CARF and SACS CASI.[13]

The school's enrollment agreement, as posted on its website in 2010, was a contract with the Academy of Swift River, a Delaware limited liability company.[14] It bound families to a minimum stay of 12 months at a program fee of $6,560 a month, plus a one-time enrollment fee of $2,500.[14] Parents transferred temporary custody of their child to the school by power of attorney, and the agreement allowed for students placed in the school's custody by a court.[14] Around 2000 Aspen Youth Services offered families tuition loans through Sallie Mae.[15]

In a statement submitted for the record of an April 24, 2008 House hearing on abuse and deceptive marketing at residential programs for teens, a parent advocate wrote that Swift River "remains unlicensed" and had "escorted the state off their property."[16] A former student wrote in 2008 that new arrivals in the "Base Camp" phase were given a ration of a bag of granola, an apple, some carrots and some celery.[17] Another, who was there shortly before it closed, wrote on Reddit in 2016 that staff ignored a warning that a friend was suicidal, and that the writer found the friend bleeding in a stairwell the next day.[18] Shane Reardon, the son of former pitcher Jeff Reardon, was sent to Swift River after his parents pulled him out of high school before his senior year; he died of a methadone overdose in his Winter Park, Florida, apartment on February 21, 2004, at 20.[19]

Swift River also figured in a special-education lawsuit.[20] A Pennsylvania student with ADHD enrolled there on August 27, 2001, and stayed through the 2001–02 school year after her parents rejected the education plan offered by the Rose Tree Media School District.[20] The family sued the district in federal court in November 2003, seeking tuition reimbursement or compensatory education; the district court ruled for the district in May 2009, and the Third Circuit affirmed in September 2010.[20]

In July 2013 Aspen announced it would close Swift River and two other Eastern boarding schools that summer, saying "the current market just does not support us continuing operations".[3] The executive director, Bar Clarke, cited changing market dynamics and families' inability to pay for treatment.[2] The school then had 55 to 65 students, and about 50 employees lost their jobs.[2] The campus now houses Swift River, a drug treatment program for adults.[21]
