SLS Residential was a private residential mental health program in Brewster, New York, owned by Joseph Santoro and Alfred Bergman.[1][2] It also ran a clinic and did business as SLS Health, SLS Wellness and Supervised Lifestyles Inc. It charged up to $950 a day.

In November 2006 inspectors from the state Office of Mental Health (OMH) found 11 violations at SLS. Patients had been restrained against their will in violation of state law, including in a room with no video surveillance. Staff had been hired without criminal background checks. The state fined SLS $110,000. In 2007 a complaint alleged that two employees, Matt Sena and Gabe Caputo, shoved patients, threw them to the ground and pinned them.[3][3]

Also in 2007, former patients filed a federal class action alleging that SLS had violated their rights under the Americans With Disabilities Act. They sought $75 million in compensatory and $150 million in punitive damages. In July 2008 the federal judge apologized for criticizing an SLS lawyer over the lobbying of former patients to opt out of the suit. In October 2008 the court fined SLS $35,000 for trying to scare former patients away from the lawsuit.

A state hearing officer found that SLS had violated patients' rights and broken the law. The OMH commissioner upheld those findings in June 2008, and the fines were upheld that July. In late August 2008 OMH revoked the company's operating licenses. The charges included illegal restraints, sedating patients who refused medication, misleading testimony and a lack of character and competence. The same year, the Commission on Accreditation of Rehabilitation Facilities (CARF) re-accredited SLS for three years.[4]

SLS sued in state Supreme Court in September 2008 to keep its licenses. That December a judge ruled in its favor, halting the state's action and canceling the $110,000 in fines. In 2011 a state appeals court confirmed the revocation and dismissed the company's challenge on the merits.
