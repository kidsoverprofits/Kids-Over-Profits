InnerChange was a Utah-based company that owned residential treatment programs, mostly for adolescent girls and young women, from 2006 until 2018.[1][2][3] That year it was folded into Embark Behavioral Health.[1][2][3] It began as Solacium Holdings and was owned in turn by two private equity firms, CIC Partners and Cressey & Company.[4][5] Its record includes a New Haven therapist, Jason Scott Calder, who pleaded guilty in November 2017 to raping a 16-year-old patient.[6]

The company took shape around two Utah treatment centers.[1] On July 1, 2006, New Haven, in Spanish Fork, bought Sunrise RTC, and both became part of Solacium Holdings, LLC.[1] In April 2007 Solacium merged with Wellcore, LLC, which owned Fulshear Ranch Academy in Texas.[7] That August Solacium, then based in Missouri City, Texas, signed a letter of intent to buy Wilderness Quest in Monticello, Utah.[8] Its chief executive, Val H. Christensen, announced that Alldredge Academy in West Virginia would drop its academic phase and become Alldredge Wilderness Journey.[8] In October 2007 Woodbury Reports listed Sommerville Young Adult Community, a program for young adults, as part of Solacium Holdings.[9] Whether the Wilderness Quest purchase closed is not on record; the company's December 2008 list of programs leaves it out.[10] By June 2008 CIC Partners of Dallas owned most of the company, which ran five programs.[4] Woodbury Reports announced that June that Anthony Geraci had resigned as Solacium's chief operations and marketing officer and that David Michel would succeed Christensen as president and chief executive.[11] In July 2008 Woodbury gave Solacium's address as Orem, Utah.[12] On November 3, 2008, its chief executive, David Michel, announced that Solacium was now InnerChange.[10] Its programs were New Haven, Sunrise, Fulshear Ranch Academy and Alldredge Wilderness Journey in West Virginia.[10] Alldredge Wilderness Journey closed in 2008.[13] In April 2009 Woodbury listed InnerChange's offices in Addison, Texas.[14] By July 2011 Dustin Tibbitts, New Haven's executive director, was also president of InnerChange.[15] In December 2011 Cressey & Company bought InnerChange from CIC Partners.[5]

Under Cressey the company expanded.[2] In 2011 Lake House Academy in Flat Rock, North Carolina, told a court in a suit against its founder that it was in a prospective sale to InnerChange.[16] On January 2, 2013, it bought Chrysalis in Eureka, Montana, "the fourth facility to join the InnerChange family."[2] That August, describing itself as "a clinical and operational consulting organization," it began a partnership with the Oakley School in Oakley, Utah.[17] InnerChange also announced that Paul Taylor, the executive director of Copper Canyon Academy, would become Oakley's executive director.[18] The Oakley School announced in April 2017 that it would close, letting students stay until June 30.[19] In March 2016 Optimum Performance Institute, a residential treatment center in Southern California for people aged 17 to 28, joined.[20] In August 2018 Calo Programs bought Dragonfly Transitions, a program for young adults in Oregon, from Glen White and Mona Treadway.[21] Cressey & Company exited InnerChange that August.[22]

In September 2017 the Utah County Sheriff's Office arrested Calder, a licensed therapist who had worked at New Haven.[23] Police said he sexually abused a 16-year-old patient during therapy sessions from March to June 2017.[23] Co-workers had noticed that his sessions with her ran late at night and included walks off the grounds, which he did not do with other patients.[23] He had been fired and was working at another treatment center in Salt Lake County.[23] In November 2017 he pleaded guilty to rape, object rape, forcible sodomy, forcible sexual abuse and obstructing justice.[6] In January 2018 he was sentenced to seven years to life in prison.[6]

In October 2018 InnerChange, Calo Programs and two other companies took the shared name Embark Behavioral Health.[24][3] New Haven, Sunrise, Chrysalis, Optimum Performance Institute, Vive and Dragonfly Transitions went into Embark under InnerChange, and the name lived on as an Embark division.[24][3] Lake House Academy and Fulshear were listed under Calo.[24] Sunrise is still registered as Solacium Sunrise, LLC, after the company's first name.[3]

After the merger, in April 2023, Oliver Whitcomb and Amy Clifford sued InnerChange Chrysalis, LLC and others in federal court in Montana, a personal-injury case.[25] The suit alleged that a staff member sexually abused students and that other staff members knew of the abuse and allowed it to continue.[26] In December 2025 the parties agreed to dismiss it with prejudice; the docket records no judgment on the merits.[25]
