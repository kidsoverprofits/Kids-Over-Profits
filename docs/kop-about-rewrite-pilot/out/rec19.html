InnerChange was a Utah-based company that owned residential treatment programs, mostly for adolescent girls and young women, from 2006 until 2018. That year it was folded into Embark Behavioral Health.[1][2][3] It began as Solacium Holdings and was owned in turn by two private equity firms, CIC Partners and Cressey & Company.[4][5] Its record includes a New Haven therapist, Jason Scott Calder, who pleaded guilty in November 2017 to raping a 16-year-old patient.[6]

The company took shape around two Utah treatment centers. On July 1, 2006, New Haven, in Spanish Fork, bought Sunrise RTC, and both became part of Solacium Holdings, LLC.[1] In April 2007 Solacium merged with Wellcore, LLC, which owned Fulshear Ranch Academy in Texas.[7] By June 2008 CIC Partners of Dallas owned most of the company, which ran five programs.[4] On November 3, 2008, its chief executive, David Michel, announced that Solacium was now InnerChange. Its programs were New Haven, Sunrise, Fulshear Ranch Academy and Alldredge Wilderness Journey in West Virginia.[8] In December 2011 Cressey & Company bought InnerChange from CIC Partners.[5]

Under Cressey the company expanded. On January 2, 2013, it bought Chrysalis in Eureka, Montana, "the fourth facility to join the InnerChange family."[2] That August, describing itself as "a clinical and operational consulting organization," it began a partnership with the Oakley School in Oakley, Utah.[9] In March 2016 Optimum Performance Institute, a residential treatment center in Southern California for people aged 17 to 28, joined.[10]

In September 2017 the Utah County Sheriff's Office arrested Calder, a licensed therapist who had worked at New Haven. Police said he sexually abused a 16-year-old patient during therapy sessions from March to June 2017. Co-workers had noticed that his sessions with her ran late at night and included walks off the grounds, which he did not do with other patients.[11] He had been fired and was working at another treatment center in Salt Lake County.[11] In November 2017 he pleaded guilty to rape, object rape, forcible sodomy, forcible sexual abuse and obstructing justice. In January 2018 he was sentenced to seven years to life in prison.[6]

In October 2018 InnerChange, Calo Programs and two other companies took the shared name Embark Behavioral Health. New Haven, Sunrise, Chrysalis, Optimum Performance Institute, Vive and Dragonfly Transitions went into Embark under InnerChange, and the name lived on as an Embark division.[3] Sunrise is still registered as Solacium Sunrise, LLC, after the company's first name.[3]

After the merger, in April 2023, Oliver Whitcomb and Amy Clifford sued InnerChange Chrysalis, LLC and others in federal court in Montana, a personal-injury case. In December 2025 the parties agreed to dismiss it with prejudice; the docket records no judgment on the merits.[12]
