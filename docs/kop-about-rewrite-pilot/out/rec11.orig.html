Rewrite

Canyon State Academy is a residential treatment center for boys in Queen Creek, Arizona, run by Rite of Passage.[1] It began as Arizona Boys Ranch, founded by Howard Pyle, Al Waters and George Miller.[2][2][1][1][1] Besides Queen Creek, the ranch ran six sites in Arizona, among them Sycamore Canyon Academy in Oracle and ranches in Payson and Williams.[2][1] Complaints about violence go back decades. In 1971 the ranch's superintendent, Wendell Newell, was accused of punching boys, and the ranch's president answered that wayward youth could not be "handled by pantywaist methods".[3] A 1982 state report described staff grabbing boys by the throat and throwing them to the ground, and Maricopa County courts removed 19 boys from the ranch.[3] In 1987, after runaways reported abuse, a state Department of Corrections investigation supported Child Protective Services' findings; the ranch disputed them.[3]

On June 27, 1994, 17-year-old Lorenzo Johnson drowned in a canal during what staff called an escape attempt.[3] Officials ruled the death accidental; his mother suggested he may have been murdered. In 1995 Alameda County, California, withdrew its 67 boys after half of them said they had been abused. The county's juvenile court concluded the abuse was systematic. That year the ranch fired two employees who had struck a 15-year-old boy 25 to 30 times.

On March 2, 1998, 16-year-old Nicholaus Contreraz died at the ranch of empyema, an infection that went undiagnosed.[4][5] Staff had accused him of faking illness, forced him to exercise as punishment and denied him medical care.[4] The Arizona Department of Economic Security (DES) concluded that he died of abuse and neglect. DES denied the ranch an operating license on August 27, 1998, then reinstated it by October 7. In October 1998 a grand jury indicted five former staff members on child abuse and manslaughter charges. Seventeen former staff were placed on the Arizona Child Abuser Directory. In July 1999 his mother received more than $1 million in civil settlements.[4] That December a judge dismissed felony child abuse charges against two former workers, after prosecutors concluded they could not be linked to his injuries.[5] Charges against others, including the nurse Linda Babb, were dismissed after expert witnesses withdrew or revised their opinions.[6]

In 2000 Arizona Boys Ranch was renamed Canyon State Academy, and Rite of Passage took it over.[1][2] Rite of Passage, a for-profit management company, runs it for a nonprofit governed by a board of directors.[2] James D. Bednark is president of both Canyon State Academy and Rite of Passage.[1] In February 2003 a 26-year-old employee was placed on leave over allegations that she had sex with a 16-year-old student.[7] Police complaints from 2000 to 2013 included alleged sexual assault of minors, child abuse and missing children. In 2014 Sycamore Canyon Academy began taking in unaccompanied migrant children.[8][9] In 2015 Maryland's juvenile justice monitors reported two boys it had sent to Canyon State were badly injured in restraints. One suffered a compression fracture of the back.

On January 21, 2020, a 17-year-old student was found unresponsive at Canyon State and died.[10][11][10] The cause was not determined, and the Maricopa County Sheriff's Office investigated.[10] On October 3, 2022, a 16-year-old student died of a suspected overdose, and two other students were hospitalized.[12][13][14] Early reports suggested family members may have supplied the drugs.[13] The school then faced scrutiny over hundreds of police calls about missing students and assaults.[15][16] Former students described a punitive environment, and a foster parent called for closer state oversight.[15][16][17][18] In July 2023 police records and Department of Child Safety files detailed staff assaulting students during restraints.[19] Several staff members were charged, some with aggravated assault.[19]
