Rewrite

Midwest Academy was a boarding school for teenagers in Keokuk, Iowa, in the network of the World Wide Association of Specialty Programs and Schools (WWASPS).[1] Benjamin Trane opened it in 2003 and became its outright owner in 2007.[1][1][1] The campus belonged to Midwest Twister LLC, a company linked to WWASPS founder Robert Lichfield, which leased it to Trane's corporation.[1][1][1] The Iowa Department of Human Services never licensed the academy or its Midwest Treatment Center in Montrose.[1][1]

Former students described small concrete isolation rooms where children were held for days or weeks. Six of them said in February 2016 that noise was piped into the boxes. A student stayed inside until sitting in a set posture for 24 hours. One former student alleged that boys who ran away in April 2015 were each held in the rooms for at least three weeks. At Trane's trial, prosecutors said two boys, aged 12 and 13, spent 133 of 210 days and 163 of 233 days in isolation. Both lost weight dramatically. Students also described frequent restraint. Lee County sheriff records showed five reports of sexual offenses at the academy over three years. They showed 73 other incidents, including runaways, assaults and suicide attempts.

A former employee sued in January 2016, saying the academy fired her in December 2015 for reporting a student's alleged sexual abuse to Human Services. On January 28, 2016, the Iowa Division of Criminal Investigation, the FBI and other agencies searched the Keokuk campus and the Montrose treatment center. A former student, 17 years old at the time, had reported that Trane sexually abused her. Investigators also examined fraud and child endangerment in the use of the isolation rooms. Human Services opened 28 child abuse assessments of students that month and later reviewed 19 founded abuse cases at the academy. The academy had applied for foster care licenses for both sites, but the application was incomplete and no site visit had taken place. Authorities closed it in February 2016. That year the Iowa Senate passed SF 2235 to certify and oversee children's residential facilities like Midwest Academy, but the House never took it up.

In March 2016 six former students and several parents sued the academy and Trane. They alleged isolation boxes, sexual harassment and abuse, inadequate medical care and filthy conditions. In May 2017 a judge awarded the fired employee $748,800 by default. In November 2017 an Iowa judge granted the families a default judgment, with damages to be set later. Midwest Academy was also named in Wood v. WWASPS, a Utah suit by several hundred former students and parents against dozens of programs.[1]

In September 2017, after a 19-month investigation, Trane was charged with third-degree sexual abuse, sexual exploitation by a counselor and child endangerment. Prosecutors alleged that he coerced a student into a sexual relationship and had girls undress in what were called "body image therapy" sessions. In December 2017 a jury acquitted him of third-degree sexual abuse. It convicted him of assault with intent to commit sexual abuse, sexual exploitation of a child by a counselor, and child endangerment. He was sentenced to nine years in prison.
