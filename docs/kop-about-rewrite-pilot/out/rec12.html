Rewrite

Falcon Ridge Ranch is a residential treatment center for girls aged 12 to 17 in Virgin, Utah.[1][2] It opened in 2003 and has passed through three owners: the Centers for Adolescent Recovery and Education (CARE), Sequel Youth and Family Services from 2016, and Rite of Passage since 2021.[3][4] In 2019 Sequel closed its sister school, Red Rock Canyon School, amid state investigations into sexual assault, violence and neglect.[5] In 2021 a Falcon Ridge staff member was charged with sexual battery of four girls.[6]

Under CARE, Falcon Ridge was built around horses, with 47 of them on 12 acres.[7] It paired the horse work with Positive Peer Culture, a confrontational group method, and Larry Brendtro, an early promoter of that approach, trained its staff.[7] It was licensed by the state.[1] In the 2009 directory of the National Association of Therapeutic Schools and Programs (NATSAP), which it had joined in 2008, Falcon Ridge listed 22 students and stays of 9 to 12 months and said its school was accredited by the Northwest Association of Accredited Schools.[8]

In August 2016 CARE sold Falcon Ridge and its sister programs, Red Rock Canyon School, Lava Heights Academy and Mount Pleasant Academy, to Sequel; Sequel described the deal to investors in a 2017 SEC filing.[4][9] The sellers were the family of Frank and Melanie Habibian, who had opened the first of the four CARE schools in St. George in 1996; their agreement with Sequel, dated August 16, 2016, set a base price of $23.5 million plus a contingent payment tied to later earnings.[10] The sellers' filing names Brian Pace as Falcon Ridge's executive director.[10] In June 2017 Sequel paid the United States and Nevada about $2.58 million to settle claims over $1.72 million in Nevada Medicaid payments for students who had been referred to the CARE schools by organizations that were then paid referral fees.[10] That October Sequel sought $2.67 million from the sellers in arbitration to cover the settlement and its costs.[10] On May 1, 2020, the arbitrator rejected Sequel's claims, found that the sellers were owed a contingent payment of at least $3.1 million, and awarded them $1.3 million in attorneys' fees and costs; the sellers then asked the Washington County court to confirm the award.[10] Red Rock Canyon came under scrutiny in 2019.[5] After a riot there on April 28, Utah investigated claims of sexual assault, violence and neglect and put its license on conditional status, and in June an employee was charged with punching a 17-year-old girl.[5] That July Sequel announced it would close Red Rock Canyon, and it also closed Mount Pleasant, which it said was unrelated.[5] Falcon Ridge's own website said in 2021 that Rite of Passage had taken it over from Sequel that October, and Utah has licensed it to Care Youth Corporation, doing business as Rite of Passage, since January 31, 2022.[4]

In early March 2021 Falcon Ridge reported a staff member, Guyla Reann Savage, to the Washington County Sheriff's Office.[11][6][12] Four students told detectives she had touched or slapped their buttocks numerous times and shown them an explicit photo of her husband on her phone.[11][6][12] She was arrested and charged with four felony counts of dealing in material harmful to minors and four misdemeanor counts of sexual battery.[11][6][12]

Former residents have also posted accounts online describing abuse, forced manual labor and medical neglect at the ranch.[13][14]
