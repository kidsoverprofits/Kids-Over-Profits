Aspen Education Group, based in Cerritos, California, owned wilderness programs, boarding schools, residential treatment centers and the Wellspring camps and academies. It was founded in 1997. We hold records of 40 programs it ran in 16 states, Washington, D.C., and England. Only one, Talisman Camps in North Carolina, is still open.

On December 16, 1998, 17-year-old Brandon Hoffman died by suicide at Aspen's Mount Bachelor Academy in Prineville, Oregon.[1] Mount Bachelor and SageWalk Wilderness, also in Oregon, both closed in 2009. At least 15 Aspen programs closed between 2009 and 2014. They included Aspen Ranch and Aspen Achievement Academy in Loa, Utah, in 2011, and the Academy at Swift River in Massachusetts in 2013. The Oakley School in Utah, owned by Aspen since 2004, ran under InnerChange from August 2013 until it closed in 2017.[2]

Other programs went to new owners and reopened under new names. Island View RTC became Elevations RTC, and the Aspen Institute for Behavioral Assessment became the Viewpoint Center. Copper Canyon Academy in Arizona became Sedona Sky Academy, and Stone Mountain School in North Carolina became Asheville Academy for Girls. Both closed in 2025. In 2014 the buyers of Island View, Turn-About Ranch, Copper Canyon and the assessment program sued Aspen. They said Aspen had kept payments owed to them after the sale; Aspen answered that the buyers had broken the agreement first. The case was dismissed with prejudice in 2015.

Also in 2014, Terri and David Myers sued Aspen, Island View, CRC Health Group, Bain Capital and the Dr. Phil organization over their daughter's placement. They alleged they had been misled into sending her to Island View, where they said she was harmed physically and psychologically. In 2015 the court barred an amended complaint and dismissed the claims against Bain and the Dr. Phil defendants with prejudice.
