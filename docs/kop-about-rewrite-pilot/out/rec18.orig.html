Now

In 2002, Securicor New Century of Richmond, Va. ran three Florida juvenile facilities, including Avon Park Youth Academy (Polk County) and Sago Palm Academy (western Palm Beach County), having taken over operations from Correctional Services Corp.; a rival bidder cited a 2000 Florida DJJ report that guards at Sago Palm abused teen inmates and shackled one to a bed for 14 hours. In 2002, Securicor had a contract for a 104-bed Florida DJJ juvenile facility and was the preferred provider for a moderate-risk residential program in Avon Park. In July 2004, G4S was formed when Group 4 Falck's security business (Group 4 Falck had bought Wackenhut in 2002) merged with Securicor. In March 2008, Florida DJJ sent G4S Youth Services a takeover notice for Hastings Youth Academy (serious incidents, inappropriate staff-youth relationships), lifted July 2008; the DJJ inspector general found staff used unnecessary force in a February 2008 incident in which a boy's arm was broken. In March 2013, Brown County, Texas approved leasing part of its juvenile justice building in Brownwood to G4S Youth Services, whose North American corporate offices were in Florida.

In August 2013, riot at Avon Park Youth Academy; 21 G4S staff on shift for 144 youths, 64 sent to Polk County Jail; G4S then ran 16 state facilities, 13 in Florida, 2 in Tennessee and 1 in Arkansas. In July 2015, a Polk County grand jury said Highlands Youth Academy, run by G4S under a $40 million five-year DJJ contract, should be closed. In May 2016, six youths escaped from the G4S-operated Okeechobee Youth Development Center. In June 2017, DJJ froze admissions at Hastings Youth Academy after a third escape in three months, citing critical deficiencies in youth supervision by G4S Youth Services. In June 2017, escape at Jacksonville Youth Academy, run by G4S Youth Services; president and CEO Martin Favis.

In April 2017, G4S completed the sale of its U.S. youth services to BHSB Holdings for $56.5 million. In August 2017, three former G4S Highlands Youth Academy administrators arrested; the sheriff said G4S staff routinely covered up fights, uses of force, contraband and escapes. Ran Stones River Academy (G4S Academy for Young Men) in Donelson, Nashville until the April 1, 2017 sale (Source: https://digital-release.wkrn.com/news/2-juveniles-captured-after-escaping-donelson-youth-facility); and G4S-The Academy for Young Women, 279 Stewarts Ferry Pike, Nashville, for DCS. In 2018 the Brown County lease was held by TrueCore, formerly known as G4S Youth Services (Source: https://www.brownwoodnews.com/2018/08/20/commissioners-vote-not-to-renew-juvenile-detention-building-lease/); TrueCore left Texas in June 2020, assigning The Oaks to Rite of Passage. In October 2019 it was a qui tam complaint against TrueCore also named G4S US, G4S Youth Services, LLC and G4S Secure Solutions as defendants.

G4S Youth Services said it had provided residential and outpatient treatment programs to more than 12,000 Florida youth since 2002. 2007 to July 2016: ran the Arkansas Juvenile Assessment and Treatment Center near Alexander; lost the $34.1 million contract to Rite of Passage; president Jim Hill and COO Brian Neupaver left in May 2016 for Youth Opportunity Investments, and G4S sued them and YOI in Florida. January-February 2016: escape from Falkenburg Academy (Tampa Residential Facility), operated for the state by G4S; first 911 call more than 13 hours later; four staff suspended.

Rewrite

G4S Youth Services was the juvenile justice arm of the security company G4S in the United States, based in Florida.[1][2] Of the 13 programs we hold, eight were in Florida. The rest were in Nashville, Tennessee; near Alexander, Arkansas; and in Brownwood, Texas. Eleven of the 13 passed to other operators rather than closing.

In August 2013, 21 staff were on shift for 144 youths when a riot broke out at Avon Park Youth Academy.[3] Afterward 64 youths were sent to the Polk County Jail.[3] In July 2015 a Polk County grand jury said Highlands Youth Academy should be closed.[4] In early 2016 a youth escaped from Falkenburg Academy in Tampa, and no one called 911 for more than 13 hours.[5] Four staff members were suspended.[5] That May six youths escaped from the Okeechobee Youth Development Center.[6] In June 2017 the state froze admissions at Hastings Youth Academy after its third escape in three months.[7] The state cited critical failures in how the company supervised youths.[7] Youths also escaped from Jacksonville Youth Academy that month.[8] In August 2017 three former Highlands administrators were arrested.[9] The sheriff said G4S staff there routinely covered up fights, uses of force, contraband and escapes.[9]

In May 2016 the company's president, Jim Hill, and chief operating officer, Brian Neupaver, left for Youth Opportunity Investments.[10] G4S sued both men and the new company in Florida.[10] That July Rite of Passage won the $34.1 million Arkansas contract G4S had held since 2007.[10] In April 2017 G4S sold its American youth business to BHSB Holdings for $56.5 million, and it carried on as TrueCore Behavioral Solutions.[9][11] TrueCore left Texas in June 2020 and handed The Oaks in Brownwood to Rite of Passage.[11]
