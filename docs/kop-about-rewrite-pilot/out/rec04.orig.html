DI Ranch LLC (a.k.a. Kayenta Homes & Properties), owner of the ranch property used by the program, is owned by Doug Caputo per HEAL. It was founded by Paul Arslanian and David Arslanian. It was owned by David Arslanian (co-owner). Hyrum W. Smith owns the property where it operates.

Its address was 115 West 1470 South, St. George, UT 84770. It cost about $800 per day. It was described as an eight-value level program with three-day seminars. Stays lasted 6 to 10 months. The minimum stay was 6 months under the 2009 enrollment agreement.

HEAL says David Arslanian started the program after being fired from Utah State University in 1999. It claims it does not take adjudicated youth or youth in foster care; HEAL says a 2008 article shows it does. Education provided through Wilostar3D (3DLearn) online school. Other high-ranking officers formerly worked for Aspen Education Group. It suspected WWASP connection; ERA denies any association.

Three co-ed Family Groups; seven-packet level system (Discovery through Respect). It uses Positive Control Systems physical intervention techniques. It uses Wilostar3D (3DLearn) online schooling as its education provider. It uses the Positive Control Systems model, same as WWASPS programs.

Rewrite

Eagle Ranch Academy is a residential treatment center for teenagers in St. George, Utah, founded by the brothers Paul and David Arslanian.[1][2][2] Its listings give 1995 as the start year; an advocacy group says David Arslanian started it after Utah State University fired him in 1999.[2] On April 26, 2005, the Washington County Planning Commission tabled the program's permit request for the DI Ranch, saying the applicants were unprepared. In May 2005 the Beaver County Commission unanimously denied it a permit for a site in the county's "grove" area. Washington County approved a permit for 20 youth or fewer on June 7, 2005.

Children move through a level system of seven "packets," from Discovery to Respect, and attend three-day seminars.[1] They live in three co-ed groups called Family Groups.[1] Staff restrain children with Positive Control Systems techniques, the same model WWASP programs used. A link to WWASP has long been suspected; the program denies any association.[1] Schooling is online, through the Wilostar3D (3DLearn) school. The program has claimed accreditation it did not hold: its earlier SAIS and SACSCOC claims were false, and it now claims CARF International.[1] It charges about $800 a day.[1] The advocacy group says it has no licensed psychologists or licensed marriage and family therapists on staff. Cantril Nielsen, a psychiatrist on its staff list, had paid a wrongful-death settlement over the 1983 suicide of a 16-year-old patient in Idaho.[3]

The same group says police complaints about the program from 2006 to 2013 include reports of sexual offenses, child abuse and children found unconscious. It said a December 2009 recording of a child talking with parents exposed cult-like seminars, beatings, undisclosed psychiatric drugs and humiliating punishments. Survivors describe violent restraints, solitary confinement as punishment, dehumanization and cult-like brainwashing. In January 2020 a parent wrote that their daughter was assaulted on her first day. An adult man then restrained her in a neck hold and put her face down on the floor; police viewed the video. On November 17, 2025, state inspectors substantiated physical abuse in an unnecessary restraint. A staff member pulled the child downward, the child's head hit the staff member's knee and then the floor, and the child suffered a concussion.
