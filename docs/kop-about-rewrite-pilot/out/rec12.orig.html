Rewrite

Falcon Ridge Ranch is a residential treatment center for girls aged 12 to 17 in Virgin, Utah, run today by Rite of Passage.[1][2] It opened in 2003 and was run by the Centers for Adolescent Recovery and Education (CARE).[3] It was built around horses, with 47 of them on 12 acres.[4] It paired the horse work with Positive Peer Culture, a confrontational group method.[4] Larry Brendtro, an early promoter of the approach, trained its staff.[4] It joined the National Association of Therapeutic Schools and Programs (NATSAP) in 2008 but appears to have left since.[5]

In August 2016 CARE sold Falcon Ridge and three sister programs to Sequel Youth and Family Services.[5][6] The three were Red Rock Canyon School, Lava Heights Academy and Mount Pleasant Academy. The sellers, led by Frank Habibian, later asked a Washington County court to confirm an arbitration award against the Sequel buyers. In July 2019 Sequel closed Red Rock Canyon School and Mount Pleasant Academy amid state and county investigations into physical and sexual abuse.[7][8] Rite of Passage took over Falcon Ridge from Sequel in October 2021.[6] Utah has licensed it to Care Youth Corporation, doing business as Rite of Passage, since January 31, 2022.[6]

A survivor wrote in April 2019 that the residential director had twisted the survivor's arm behind the back to cause pain as a restraint. In 2020 Utah regulators received reports that a staff member, Guyla Reann Savage, had slapped residents' buttocks and shown them a photo of her husband's genitals. She was fired, and the state took no action against the program. In March 2021 she was arrested and charged with felony dealing in material harmful to minors and misdemeanor sexual battery involving four students.[9][10][11] Survivors have also reported sexual assault, forced manual labor and medical neglect.
