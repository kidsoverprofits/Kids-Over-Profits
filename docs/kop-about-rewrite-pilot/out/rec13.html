Rewrite

Lava Heights Academy is a residential treatment center in Toquerville, Utah, run since October 2021 by Rite of Passage.[1] It opened in 2012, took both boys and girls for most of its history, and now serves girls.[2][3] It has passed through three owners, including Sequel Youth and Family Services, which closed two of its other Utah programs amid abuse investigations in 2019.[4][1][5] Former employees complained publicly about how it treated children as early as 2018, and in 2023 Paul Anthony Nichols, a staff member there, was sent to prison for sexually abusing two girls he supervised.[6][7]

The Centers for Adolescent Recovery and Education (CARE) built Lava Heights and opened it in 2012 as a treatment program centered on the performing arts.[2][8] It sits at 730 Spring Drive in Toquerville.[9] In 2016 Sequel Youth and Family Services acquired it along with three other Utah programs, Falcon Ridge Ranch, Red Rock Canyon School and Mount Pleasant Academy.[5] In July 2019 Sequel closed Red Rock Canyon School and Mount Pleasant Academy amid investigations into abuse.[10][5] Sequel ran Lava Heights until the fall of 2021: its website still named Sequel on September 28, 2021, and by October 20 it named Rite of Passage as the operator.[4][1] Utah has licensed it to Care Youth Corporation, doing business as Rite of Passage, since January 31, 2022.[9]

In February 2018 a former employee wrote on Yelp that management told staff how to word restraint incident reports, that reports of medication errors disappeared, and that staff who raised concerns were let go; the employee said incidents reported to Sequel's compliance manager went nowhere.[6] That same month The Spectrum reported that two boys had run away from Lava Heights and were missing for nearly four days.[11] One of their mothers said staff did not file a missing-person report or ask police to publicize the disappearance.[11] In July 2020 another former staff member wrote on Indeed: "They hurt kids and blame staff."[12]

In September 2022 Lava Heights hired Paul Anthony Nichols, 22, of St. George, to supervise girls in the program.[3][13] Three days later he started a game of "truth or dare" with students he supervised.[3][13] Two girls under 16 told detectives he touched them inappropriately on at least eight occasions, including sexual abuse in the facility's restrooms, and that he photographed one of them partly undressed.[3][13] Officials received the first report on October 20, 2022, and Nichols was arrested in December and charged with rape, forcible sexual abuse and sexual exploitation of a minor.[14][3] He admitted to investigators that he had touched the girls on multiple occasions and photographed one.[13] On March 28, 2023, he pleaded guilty to five counts of forcible sexual abuse and two of sexual exploitation of a minor, and the rape charge was dismissed.[7] On May 16, 2023, Judge Jay Winward sentenced him to 1 to 15 years in prison on each count, to run concurrently, and ordered him to register as a sex offender.[7]

State inspectors have kept finding violations since Rite of Passage took over.[9] On February 1, 2024, a licensor found two staff members supervising a group of 12 clients, out of the required ratio.[9] In September 2024 the program was cited, as a repeat violation, for not reporting private placements to the state on time.[9] In April 2025 an investigation found hand sanitizer left where clients could reach it, and one client drank it.[9] In June 2025 an investigation substantiated that a staff member fell asleep on duty and failed to supervise clients, a repeat of a violation first noted on October 12, 2022.[9] On April 29, 2026, an investigation substantiated that an unsupervised client was able to run away from the facility unnoticed.[9] The state rated the violation "Extreme" and fined the program $500.[9] On October 22, 2026, a licensor verified that the program had given the staff member a disciplinary write-up.[9]
