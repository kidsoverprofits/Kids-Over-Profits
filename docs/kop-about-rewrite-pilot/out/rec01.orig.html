Rewrite

Aspen Ranch was a residential treatment center for teenagers in Loa, Utah, run by Aspen Education Group, a division of CRC Health Group.[1][2][2][3] It opened in 1995 and belonged to NATSAP from 1999 until it closed in 2011.[4] Boys and girls lived in seven cottages and moved up through levels such as Rider and Wrangler, which earned trips off campus.[4][2] Families paid $350 a day for a minimum stay of nine months.[2][2] The first payment was $18,715, including a $7,982.50 fee for transition services that the program kept if the child left early.[2]

For about the first eight weeks, contact with home was cut off, with family letters and visits limited. A former student said staff read letters before they were sent, ended calls when students described mistreatment, and threatened transfer to a program abroad. At intake, the enrollment agreement called for a strip search and a search of all belongings. It allowed staff to restrain, control and detain students, including to bring back runaways. An advocacy group alleged that all students were subjected to body cavity searches.

Staff restrained students with the Positive Control System, using pressure points, bent wrists and locked joints.[2] A former student said the holds lasted 45 minutes to an hour or longer, and students ended up with broken wrists. Another said that when a student refused to go out to work, staff pulled the bedsheets out from under them. Staff then carried the student, threw them onto a staircase and dragged them to the work field. A former student said the psychiatrist prescribed from staff behavior reports without seeing students or checking records; one student's Ritalin prescription led to seizures. Survivors also describe overmedication, isolation and forced manual labor.

A staff list marks Scott Peterson, a floating staff member, as a convicted sex offender. In 2014 Scott Andrew Peterson, a licensed clinical social worker, was sentenced to 1 to 15 years for forcible sexual abuse of a 16-year-old client. That abuse took place at Therapy on the Fly.

In March 2011 Aspen Education Group announced it was closing or consolidating five programs, and blamed declining demand.[5] Aspen Ranch closed that year, and its program was folded into Outback Therapeutic Expeditions.[4]
