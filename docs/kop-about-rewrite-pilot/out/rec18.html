Rewrite

G4S Youth Services was the American juvenile justice arm of the British security company G4S. From offices in Florida it ran state-contracted lockups and treatment programs for youths in Florida, Tennessee, Arkansas and Texas.[1][2][3] In 2002 some of the Florida facilities it later ran, including Avon Park Youth Academy, were run by Securicor New Century, a subsidiary of a British company; one of Securicor's three Florida sites had been the subject of a state report that guards abused teenagers and shackled one to a bed for 14 hours.[4][2] For 15 years its facilities drew state sanctions and criminal investigations. In 2008 Florida declared it in default at Hastings Youth Academy after a youth's arm was broken in a restraint.[5] In 2013 a riot broke out at Avon Park Youth Academy.[2] In 2015 a grand jury called for Highlands Youth Academy to be closed.[6] Escapes followed at four of its Florida sites.[7][8][9][10] In April 2017 G4S sold the business, which carried on as TrueCore Behavioral Solutions.[11][12]

The Florida contracts go back to Securicor. In 2002 Securicor New Century, of Richmond, Virginia, a subsidiary of the British firm Securicor, ran three Florida juvenile facilities, among them Avon Park Youth Academy in Polk County and Sago Palm Academy in western Palm Beach County. It had taken over operations from Correctional Services Corp.[4] That August, when Nevada chose Securicor to run a youth prison, the losing bidder, Rite of Passage, pointed to a 2000 Florida Department of Juvenile Justice report that guards at Sago Palm had abused teenagers, shackled one to a bed for 14 hours and used a choke hold. It also cited a 2001 state report that rated Avon Park's programs below average. Securicor's chief executive, Gail Browne, said early problems had been corrected.[4] G4S Youth Services later said it had served more than 12,000 Florida youths since 2002.[7]

The division grew mainly through Florida Department of Juvenile Justice contracts. By August 2013 it ran 16 state facilities: 13 in Florida, two in Tennessee and one in Arkansas.[2] The Arkansas site was the state's Juvenile Assessment and Treatment Center near Alexander, which it ran from 2007.[13] In Nashville it ran the G4S Academy for Young Men, also called Stones River Academy, a program for 24 boys committed to the Tennessee Department of Children's Services.[3] In March 2013 Brown County, Texas, agreed to lease part of its juvenile justice building in Brownwood to the company.[1]

Its record in Florida went bad early. After a February 2008 incident, a Hastings Youth Academy employee, Kutanna Woods, was fired for using unnecessary force on a youth, who suffered a broken shoulder. His lawyer said surveillance video showed her twisting his arm behind his back until it broke after he refused to sweep a floor. The department's inspector general found that staff used unnecessary force, but the sheriff declined to file charges.[5] In March 2008 the department declared G4S in default of its Hastings contract, citing serious incidents, inappropriate staff-youth relationships and no effective behavior management system. It lifted the notice that July, saying the company had substantially complied.[5]

The problems continued for years. In August 2013, 21 staff were on shift for 144 youths when a riot broke out at Avon Park Youth Academy, and 64 youths were sent to the Polk County Jail.[2] In July 2015 a Polk County grand jury said Highlands Youth Academy, which G4S ran under a five-year, $40 million state contract, should be closed.[6] In early 2016 a youth escaped from Falkenburg Academy in Tampa, and no one called 911 for more than 13 hours; four staff members were suspended.[7] That May six youths escaped from the Okeechobee Youth Development Center.[8] In June 2017 the state froze admissions at Hastings after its third escape in three months, citing critical failures in how the company supervised youths.[9] Youths escaped from Jacksonville Youth Academy that month too.[10] In August 2017 three former Highlands administrators were arrested. The sheriff said G4S staff there had routinely covered up fights, uses of force, contraband and escapes.[11]

By then G4S was leaving the business. In May 2016 the division's president, Jim Hill, and Brian Neupaver, who ran its operations, left for a rival, Youth Opportunity Investments, and G4S sued both men and the new company in Florida.[13] That July Rite of Passage won the $34.1 million Arkansas contract G4S had held since 2007.[13] On April 1, 2017, G4S sold its American youth business to BHSB Holdings for $56.5 million, and it was renamed TrueCore Behavioral Solutions.[11][3][12] TrueCore left Texas in June 2020 and handed The Oaks in Brownwood to Rite of Passage.[12]
