Eagle Ranch Academy is a licensed residential treatment center for teenagers at 115 West 1470 South in St. George, Utah, with room for 57 young people.[1] It was founded by the brothers Paul and David Arslanian, former Utah State University football coaches.[2][3] Police logged calls to the academy reporting assaults, child abuse and runaways from 2006 on, and former students and parents have published accounts of the program since 2013 (see Survivor testimony below).[4][5][6][7]

David Arslanian was Utah State's head football coach in 1998 and 1999.[3] He hired his brother Paul as a coordinator over the university president's objection, was fired on December 2, 1999, and sued the university in 2000.[3] By 2013 Woodbury Reports, an industry newsletter, listed David as the academy's co-founder and director of admissions.[2] Paul Arslanian was the person on site when state licensers inspected the program in August 2025.[1] Woodbury Reports listed 1995 as the program's start year.[2]

In 2005 the program sought land-use permits in two southern Utah counties.[8][9] Seven years earlier the Salt Lake Tribune had reported that Hyrum Smith, the founder of Franklin Quest, owned the D.I. Ranch, an 831-acre cattle ranch 25 miles west of St. George, and planned to sell it to investors who wanted to turn it into a wilderness retreat for troubled youths.[10] On April 26, 2005, the Washington County Planning Commission took up a request to run a residential treatment facility and school for 20 youths at "Eagle Ranch Academy, Inc. (a.k.a. DI Ranch)," with Dave Arslanian as one of the applicant's agents.[8] The commission's chairman said the applicants were not prepared and needed to provide much more information, and the request was tabled.[8] In May 2005 the Beaver County Commission, after hearing Dave Arslanian, unanimously upheld its planning commission's denial of a permit for the academy in the county's "grove" area.[9] On June 7, 2005, the Washington County Commission approved the D.I. Ranch permit for 20 youths or fewer, on the applicant's statements that each youth would stay six to ten months and that no adjudicated youth would be admitted.[11] The St. George Area Chamber of Commerce later listed Doug Caputo as the contact for D.I. Ranch LLC, also known as Kayenta Homes & Properties.[12]

The academy's 2009 enrollment packet told parents it was licensed by the Utah Department of Human Services.[13] It bound families to a minimum stay of six months while reserving the program's right to dismiss a student at any time, and it authorized staff to restrain students with Positive Control Systems techniques.[13] The program has publicly denied any association with WWASP.[14]

State licensers have repeatedly found the program out of compliance.[15][16][17] In March 2024 an investigation substantiated that staff had left clients unsupervised for longer than 15 minutes, during which they engaged in unsafe behaviors, a repeat violation.[15] That May licensers found the program had not reported a May 4 critical incident until May 7, also a repeat violation.[16] On November 17, 2025, an investigation found it out of compliance with the rules requiring providers to protect clients from abuse and to follow safe practices.[17]

Complaints about the academy reach back years before the survivors' posts.[4] St. George police records of incidents at its address from 2006 to 2013 include reports of sexual offenses, child abuse and children found unconscious, along with assaults and runaway juveniles.[4] A recording of a December 2009 conversation between a student and the student's parents at the academy was later published online.[18]
