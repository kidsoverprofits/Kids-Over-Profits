Aspen Ranch was a residential treatment center for teenagers in Loa, Utah, run by Aspen Education Group, a division of CRC Health Group, until 2011.[1][2] It combined a working horse ranch with a school.[3] A former student there in 2008 and 2009 has said staff pinned teenagers face down when they refused orders and that one staff member broke a boy's wrist.[4] Aspen Education Group closed the ranch in 2011, saying demand for therapeutic programs had fallen.[2]

Its 2009 listing with the National Association of Therapeutic Schools and Programs gave 1995 as its founding year and 1999 as the year it joined.[5] The ranch was operating by 2000. That September it had 55 students aged 13 to 17, with room for 70; new arrivals lived in a building called "Roundup," and students moved up through levels named Mustang, Maverick, Greenhorn and Rancher.[3] Kreg Gillman was executive director, and the average stay was about five months.[3] The Utah Department of Human Services licensed it as a residential treatment center.[3][6]

By 2009 the ranch listed 80 students aged 13 to 18, stays of six to 12 months, and Melodie Rose as its executive contact.[5]

A former student who was there in 2008 and 2009 described the ranch on Unsilenced, a site that publishes survivor accounts. The student said staff pinned teenagers face down when they refused orders. The student said a staff member, Joe Giles, broke a boy's wrist taking him to the ground, and that the boy waited hours for care.[4]

On March 24, 2011, Aspen Education Group announced it would discontinue five programs, Aspen Ranch among them, and consolidate three others.[2] The company cited "the reduced demand for therapeutic schools and programs in today's economy."[2] Aspen Ranch closed that year; Aspen Achievement Academy, the company's wilderness program in the same town, was merged into Outback Therapeutic Expeditions.[2]
