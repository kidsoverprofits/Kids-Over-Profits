Colorado County Boot Camp, later called the Colorado County Juvenile Facility, was a secure facility for juvenile offenders in Eagle Lake, Texas. Correctional Services Corporation (CSC), a private prison company, opened it in 1998, and after CSC bought Youth Services International (YSI) in 1999 the facility ran under YSI.[1][2][3] It held youth from several Texas counties and the state, growing from 100 to 156 beds by 2002.[1][2] On August 31, 2002, a youth died there in what authorities called a suicide.[4]

CSC opened the boot camp in 1998 as a 100-bed county detention facility, which it managed without owning the property.[1] YSI was a separate company: W. James Hindman, the founder of Jiffy Lube, started it in 1991, opened its first program, Clarinda Academy in Iowa, in 1992, and took it public in 1994.[5] CSC acquired YSI in 1999, and from then on CSC's juvenile programs, Eagle Lake among them, operated under YSI's name; YSI's website listed the Eagle Lake facility in 2003.[2][3] By 2002 the camp had 156 beds and held youth under a contract with several counties and the state. CSC owned part of the site, an expansion it had built, and managed the rest.[2]

On August 31, 2002, a youth died at the boot camp. Authorities called it a "clear case of suicide" and did not release the youth's name. Prison Legal News reported the death in a story on deaths and settlements at YSI facilities.[4] In the fourth quarter of 2004 CSC wrote down the value of the facility by $1.5 million, saying its assets were not recoverable, and in March 2005 it still listed Eagle Lake as a 156-bed secure facility under a state and county contract.[6] The GEO Group completed its purchase of CSC on November 7, 2005, and sold YSI for $3.75 million to a new company owned by James F. Slattery, CSC's former chief executive.[7] YSI went on running juvenile programs under its own name. In March 2016 Florida's Department of Juvenile Justice agreed to end its seven contracts with YSI in settling a whistleblower suit by former employees.[8]
