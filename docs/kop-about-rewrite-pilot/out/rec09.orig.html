Rewrite

Mount Bachelor Academy was a therapeutic boarding school for teenagers near Prineville, Oregon.[1] Linda Houghton founded it with other former CEDU employees; Steve Houghton, Alex Bitz and Greg Andrick are also named as founders.[1][1][1][1] It was owned by College Health Enterprises, which was spun off into the company that became Aspen Education Group.[1] Aspen was owned by CRC Health Group, which in turn was owned by the private equity firm Bain Capital.[2][3]

In March 1998 four former employees and two parents accused the school of sleep deprivation, obscene tirades and overnight work in near-freezing cold. Oregon's Department of Human Services investigated and cleared it. On December 16, 1998, Brandon Hoffman, 17, died by hanging at the school.[4] Reports said he was found outside the building; survivors dispute that.

In March 2009 the department opened an investigation centered on the school's Lifesteps seminars. In April 2009 TIME, citing students, parents and a part-time employee, reported that girls were made to give boys lap dances as therapy. One girl was made to dress as a French maid. On November 3, 2009, the department confirmed nine allegations of abuse and neglect involving five students since 2007, most in the Lifesteps program. Investigators found students were made to act out sexual roles in front of staff and other students and were subjected to degrading comments. The state suspended the school's license and gave Aspen 72 hours to remove the students. CRC Health filed a mass layoff notice, and the school closed in December 2009 without reopening.[5][6] Aspen and the state reached a settlement in October 2010.[7]

On July 6, 2011, nine former students sued in Multnomah County for $14.25 million, alleging emotional, physical and sexual abuse in the 1990s.[8][9][10][6] Their complaint described sexual role-play, forced labor, sleep deprivation, exposure to extreme cold, and denial of food and medical care.[9][10][11] The school issued a statement denying the claims and saying it had provided a nurturing environment.[5] By late August the suit had 17 plaintiffs seeking $25.5 million from the school, Aspen and CRC Health.[12][13][14] In November 2011, 14 more former students sued for $23 million, in a case set for trial in Crook County.[11] Two more suits followed in 2012, one naming Alex Bitz and the executive director, Sharon Bitz, as defendants.[15] In all, 51 former students sought more than $48 million. The cases were dismissed with prejudice, and one was reportedly settled privately in 2015.

In 2016 the Christmas Valley Community Church bought the campus for $950,000; it is now the Ochoco Christian Conference Center.[1]
