David LePere directed both CEDU High School and CEDU Middle School, then ran Wilderness Quest and Cherokee Creek Boys School. The network map also lists him as a NATSAP board president. He carries the CEDU lineage, which descends from Synanon, into the post-CEDU industry and its trade association; the map shows 7 links across 4 networks.1, 2

Roles and timeline

  • ~1992 onward: 15 years in the field by 2007, including Mount Bachelor Academy (OR).1
  • Before 2005: director of CEDU High School and CEDU Middle School (Running Springs, CA).1
  • Before 2007: executive director of Wilderness Quest (Monticello, UT), billed as the country's longest-running wilderness therapy program.1
  • Nov. 2007: executive director of Cherokee Creek Boys School (Westminster, SC), a NATSAP school for middle-school boys.1
  • Undated: NATSAP board president (per the network map).2
  • Undated: board of directors, Compass Rose Academy (per the network map).2

Programs and their histories

  • CEDU High School and Middle School: CEDU, founded by Mel Wasserman on Synanon's model, used confrontational "raps" and emotional "propheet" workshops. The CEDU schools closed in 2005 when the company went bankrupt.3
  • Wilderness Quest: founded by Larry Wells, who said he had run wilderness programs for troubled teens since 1971.1
  • Cherokee Creek Boys School: founded in 2003 by Beth and Ron Black for boys with ADHD, autism, anxiety and learning disabilities. The map files it in the Aspiro frame.1, 2

Connections

  • Spouse: Janine Riehl LePere, linked on the map to Cherokee Creek and Mount Bachelor Academy.2
  • CEDU lineage: Mel Wasserman; Joe Ricci (the map's Brown Schools/CEDU frame).
  • NATSAP leadership: Kimball DeLaMare, Jan Moss, Rosemary Tippett and the other presidents profiled in this series.
  • Map links: CEDU High School and CEDU Middle School (director), Wilderness Quest and Cherokee Creek (executive director), NATSAP (board president) and Compass Rose Academy (board).2
