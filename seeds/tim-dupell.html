Tim Dupell is an accountant who was Aspen Education Group's CFO and then founded Family Help & Wellness in 2008. FHW grew by taking over programs other companies had closed or sold, many of them former Aspen programs. FHW owns Trails Carolina, where a 12-year-old died in February 2024.2, 3

Roles and timeline

  • Early career: CPA consultant at Ernst & Young; management accounting at HMO Oregon/Capital Health Care; CFO of The Corvallis Clinic; CFO of Physician Partners, Inc.1
  • 1999-2004: executive VP and CFO of Aspen Education Group.2
  • 2008: founded Family Help & Wellness in Salem, OR.2
  • 2014: private equity firm Trinity Hunt Partners invested in FHW.2
  • 2008-2017: FHW CEO; succeeded by Steve Stradley in 2017.3
  • As of Oct. 2024: his holding company, Opal Creek Capital, LLC, is listed as a member of WTC Holdco, LLC, one of the FHW entities named in the Trails lawsuits.5

How FHW grew

FHW took over programs as Aspen and others shed them. By 2021, testimony to the Oregon Senate counted 21 FHW programs across Utah, Arizona, North Carolina, Idaho and Colorado. Examples include Island View (now Elevations RTC), the Aspen Institute for Behavioral Assessment (now Viewpoint Center) and Willow Glen (now Asheville Academy).4

FHW describes itself as providing management, financial and marketing support to programs with their own separate ownership teams, as with Elevations. That structure spreads legal exposure across LLCs.3, 4

Documented harms at FHW programs

  • Trails Carolina: a 12-year-old died on his first night in February 2024. The death was ruled a homicide; no criminal charges have been filed. North Carolina removed all children and permanently revoked the license in May 2024.5
  • Regulatory citations: NBC News reported in 2024 that several FHW programs were cited for failing to report critical incidents and for preventing children from contacting licensing agencies.6
  • Class actions: two October 2024 federal suits in North Carolina seek refunds for FHW families nationwide.5

Connections

  • Fellow WTC Holdco members: Sue Crowell (PGO, LLC), Wayne Laird, Johnny Deblock.5

  • FHW leadership: Steve Stradley (CEO from 2017). Oregon testimony says both men were involved with an elder care company before FHW.4
