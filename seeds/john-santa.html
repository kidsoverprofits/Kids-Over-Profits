John L. Santa co-founded the National Association of Therapeutic Schools and Programs (NATSAP) in January 1999 and served as its first president. He co-owned Montana Academy and founded PAARP, a parent-advocate organization. He co-wrote the 2019 NATSAP history with Jan Moss Courtney.1, 3, 5

For the fuller documented account of Santa and Montana Academy, see the Kids Over Profits Investigatory Spotlight article at /investigatory-spotlight-john-santa-montana-academy/ (already published on the site).

Roles and timeline

  • 1997: helped create Montana Academy, which ran until 2021.1, 4
  • 1999: co-founded NATSAP with John Reddan, Len Buccellato and Jan Moss; became its first board president.3
  • 2006: as NATSAP president, used his newsletter column to set out a public-relations strategy against the Miller oversight bill, and signed NATSAP's open letter to its critics.5, 6
  • Undated: founded PAARP; the map links him to Montana Academy (co-owner) and NATSAP (president).1

NATSAP's political defense

In early 2006, facing what he called "the pending possibility of a federal regulatory bill (the Miller bill)," Santa's president's column laid out a media and grassroots strategy: get local stories written naming NATSAP membership, enlist satisfied parents and students, and coordinate a single message to legislators. That year NATSAP's public relations committee hired a PR firm to run a media workshop.5

In 2006 NATSAP published an open letter to its critics, signed by Santa. Dr. Allison Pinto told Congress in 2007 that it described the abuse reports she and other researchers had presented "as 'the noisy complaints of a few individuals.'" Rep. George Miller answered at the same hearing: "this cannot be dismissed as noise."6
