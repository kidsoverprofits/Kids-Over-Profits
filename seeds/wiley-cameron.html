Wiley Cameron was Lester Roloff's right-hand man and succeeded him as head of the Roloff Homes after Roloff died in 1982. He kept the homes running across state lines when Texas shut them down, then helped write the 1997 Texas law that let them return without a state license - the bridge between Roloff's original fight and the modern religious exemption.1, 2

Roles and timeline

  • 1982: succeeded Roloff as head of the Rebekah Home for Girls and the Roloff Homes.1
  • 1985: Texas prevailed in court and the Rebekah Home closed in Texas; the homes moved to Missouri.1
  • 1987: two days after a Kansas City Times investigation of physical abuse at the Missouri homes, Cameron shut them and returned to Texas.1
  • 1985-1997: lobbied the Texas Legislature for an alternative to state licensing.2
  • 1997: helped draft and lobby for HB 2482, which let faith-based children's homes be accredited privately; sat on the board of the new accrediting body, TACCCA. Bush invited the Roloff Homes back, and they were the first TACCCA-approved program.2, 3
  • June 1999: Texas CPS found physical abuse and medical neglect at the Rebekah Home and banned his wife, Fay Cameron, head of the home, from working with juveniles in Texas.2
  • 1999: resigned from the TACCCA board soon after.2
  • 2001: closed the Rebekah Home after the Legislature let the exemption law lapse; only 7 of 2,015 faith-based child care providers had used it.1, 4

Methods

Texas Monthly reported that by the later years, kneeling punishments had stopped and paddling was used "only sparingly," likely because of the 1987 Missouri exposé. Survivors describe lockup rooms where taped prayers from Roloff or Cameron played around the clock.1

After the Roloff Homes

One source says Wiley and Fay Cameron moved to Florida and opened New Beginnings. Another says former Rebekah staff helped found New Beginnings Girls Academy in Missouri. Treat both as unconfirmed.5

Connections

  • Family: wife Fay Cameron, head of the Rebekah Home.2
  • Lineage: Lester Roloff, and Thelma Ford of New Bethany, where Rebekah girls were boarded during the Texas fight.
  • Politics: Gov. George W. Bush's faith-based task force; TACCCA co-figure Pastor David Blaser.2, 3
  • Map: Cameron is not on the board yet. Add him to the Roloff frame.
