Research draft - not for publication. This page combines an archived institutional history, contemporary news reporting and an investigative article. Allegations, witness accounts, agency charges and legal rulings are identified separately; primary records still need review.

Matthew Lewis Israel founded the Behavior Research Institute (BRI) in 1971; it was renamed the Judge Rotenberg Center in 1994. The archived history says the center moved to Canton in 1996.1

In Ric Kahn's 1985 profile, Israel said he earned a psychology doctorate at Harvard in 1960, studied under B. F. Skinner, and began BRI after developing a behavior-modification program at Bradley Hospital. Kahn describes the institute as growing from Israel's effort to establish a behaviorist community and then a school for children with severe support needs. These biographical details are reported in the article and should be checked against university and institutional records.4

Contemporary scrutiny of BRI

Kahn's November 1985 investigation documents a long-running dispute over BRI's use of aversive behavior modification and the oversight of its Providence school and affiliated residential programs. It recounts a 1973 Rhode Island human-rights committee report, a 1979 New York state team's criticism of the program, and licensing and funding disputes involving BRI's California branch. These are the article's accounts of particular investigations and proceedings, not a single adjudicated finding about every site or period.4

For California, Kahn reported that the state filed an administrative accusation in 1982 alleging more than 100 licensing and special-permit violations at BRI of California, including misuse of aversives and restraints. The article says the state and the institute settled before the scheduled hearing, with physical aversives, restraints and food used as reward or punishment barred under the agreement. The accusation's claims should not be treated as findings after trial; the settlement and underlying administrative record need direct review.4

In Massachusetts, Kahn reported that the Office for Children issued an emergency measure on September 26, 1985, suspending licenses for seven BRI group homes after a seven-month review. The agency charged that the operation jeopardized residents' health and safety through food deprivation, excessive punishment and regulatory violations. At the October appeal hearing, the magistrate upheld the emergency suspension but ruled against the state on its findings of fact; the article says the full license-revocation hearing had not yet occurred. The agency charges therefore must not be described as final findings of abuse.4

The article also reported that resident Vincent Milletich died in July 1985 after staff restrained him during an episode they described as aggressive behavior. Kahn said the Bristol County district attorney's office was investigating at publication. The article does not establish that the restraint caused his death; the medical examiner, investigative and court records are needed before making any causal or legal claim.4

Kahn reported that Israel told him he had never used electric shock and would not rule it out if other procedures were unavailable. This is Israel's reported statement in 1985; it is not by itself evidence of when or how electric shock was later introduced at JRC.4

Reported practices in the intake notes

The archived JRC history describes electric-shock aversives delivered using a graduated electronic decelerator (GED), food deprivation, restraint and punishment. A California investigation into the institute's branch reported physical and psychological abuse, concealment of bruising, inadequate food and restrictions on aversives and restraints. These sources concern different sites and periods; attribute each practice accordingly and distinguish allegations from regulatory findings.1, 3

Events and legal matters to verify

  • A WBUR report describes the 2007 hoax-call incident involving two teenagers and the subsequent criminal investigation. Check the charging record before stating the exact number of shocks or Israel's role.2
  • Prosecutors alleged Israel ordered surveillance recordings destroyed; the indictment and disposition are needed to distinguish the allegation from the court's findings.2
  • In 2011 Israel left JRC under a deferred prosecution agreement after being indicted on charges related to resident abuse, according to the archived history and WBUR. The exact charge language and final disposition should be cited to the agreement and docket.1, 2
  • Source needed: the supplied notes refer to civil suits alleging abuse, wrongful death and human-rights violations, but no case names or dockets are identified here.
