Rosemary Tippett came out of Three Springs, Inc. and became NATSAP's public relations director in 2006. In that role she helped lead the industry's lobbying against Rep. George Miller's federal oversight bill. Her significance is less about running programs than about building the trade group's political defense.2, 3

Roles and timeline

  • 1995: linked to the Three Springs Atlanta office.1
  • 2002-2004: NATSAP board member representing Three Springs; chaired the Member Services committee in 2004.2
  • 2003: served on the NATSAP national conference committee.2
  • 2006: became NATSAP Director of Public Relations (also listed as Public Affairs).3
  • 2007: co-led NATSAP Advocacy Day in Washington with Jan Moss.4

Lobbying against federal oversight

In 2006, NATSAP president John Santa wrote that Tippett contacted the National Association of Psychiatric Health Systems to form an alliance. The stated goal was to limit the impact of the Miller bill, which Santa called potentially "extremely adverse" for member programs.5

That year she, Jan Moss and Karen Fitzhugh met with staff for at least six House members, including Miller's committee. They offered NATSAP as a resource on child safety and invited staff to tour member programs. The newsletter records Rep. Pete Stark saying he preferred state over federal regulation.3

On May 10, 2007, she and Moss led 36 member representatives from 15 states to meet with more than 50 legislators. The message was "realistic, practical, workable" policy, framed as protecting the family unit.4

Connections

  • Three Springs: acquired by Jay Ripley's Sequel in 2009, two years after Advocacy Day. Fellow Three Springs executive Sharon Laney was NATSAP vice president in 2006 and president in 2007.
  • NATSAP leadership: Jan Moss (Spring Ridge Academy), John Santa (Montana Academy, PAARP) and Kimball DeLaMare (Island View).
  • Map links: Three Springs Inc. (unlabelled) and NATSAP (board member).
