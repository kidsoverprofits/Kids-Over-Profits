W. Kimball DeLaMare was NATSAP's founding president. Before that, he ran KIDS of Greater Salt Lake, the 1989 Utah branch of Miller Newton's Straight spin-off, which faced assault and unlawful detention allegations within months of opening. He then co-founded Island View and the Oakley School - a direct line from the Straight/KIDS model to the trade association that represents the modern industry.1, 2, 4

Roles and timeline

  • 1989: trained for six months at KIDS of Bergen County (Hackensack, NJ), then directed KIDS of Greater Salt Lake.1
  • 1994: co-founded Island View RTC (Syracuse, UT) with Jared Balmer and Lorin Broadbent; served as executive director.2
  • ~1998: co-founded the Oakley School.3
  • 1999: founding board president of NATSAP; Tim Brace was vice president and Jan Moss secretary.4
  • 2004: Island View and Oakley sold to Aspen Education Group; DeLaMare received NATSAP's National Leadership Award.5
  • 2008: left after 13+ years to become VP of Proficio Management Group, supporting CALO (Ken Huey), Randy Oakley's program and Renovo.6
  • Later: senior VP of Potomac Programs (Embark); chief clinical officer of Life Launch Centers; BestNotes advisory board.7, 8

The KIDS of Greater Salt Lake case

In 1989, three months after the program opened, a 19-year-old sued for $6.5 million. Her lawyer said she had left, was taken off the street by a group including her parents and a man with a badge, and was returned. She broke an arm and a foot falling from a second-story roof while trying to escape.1

South Salt Lake police investigated complaints of assault and unlawful detention. A detective said the charges were "the exact same" as those against KIDS programs in El Paso and Southern California. DeLaMare did not return calls; Newton defended the program.1

Influence

As NATSAP's founding president, DeLaMare helped write its Ethical Principles and Principles of Good Practice - the self-policing standards NATSAP offers in place of outside regulation. He says he has visited more than 350 programs and helped craft licensing policy in several states. He also sat on the Joint Commission's youth advisory council.4, 7
