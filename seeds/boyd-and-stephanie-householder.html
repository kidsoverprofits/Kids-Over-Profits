Boyd and Stephanie Householder founded Circle of Hope Girls' Ranch near Humansville, Missouri, in 2006, after Boyd worked at Agape Boarding School. Missouri left faith-based boarding schools unlicensed, so the state did not oversee it. In 2020 state agencies removed about two dozen girls, and the Householders were charged with nearly 100 counts between them. Boyd died before trial; Stephanie pleaded guilty in 2025. Their case, along with Agape's, drove Missouri's 2021 oversight law.1, 2, 3, 4

Roles and timeline

  • Before 2006: Boyd, a Marine Corps Vietnam veteran, worked at reform schools in Missouri and Florida, including Agape, where the map lists him as dean of staff and ranch manager.1
  • July 2006: opened Circle of Hope Girls' Ranch in Cedar County, MO.1
  • Aug. 2020: Child Protective Services removed about two dozen girls; the ranch closed. Their daughter Amanda Householder publicly backed the survivors.2
  • Sept. 2020 onward: former residents began filing civil suits.3
  • Mar. 2021: both arrested. Boyd was charged with 79 felonies and 1 misdemeanor, including statutory rape and sodomy involving one girl; Stephanie with 22 counts of child abuse, neglect and endangerment.4
  • 2023: Stephanie rejected a plea deal that required testifying against Boyd.5
  • June 2024: Boyd died of a heart attack after pneumonia before trial, maintaining his innocence.6
  • Sept. 2025: Stephanie pleaded guilty to 6 counts of child abuse or neglect and 7 of endangering a child: 120 days with credit for time served and 5 years' probation.3

What residents described

Sixteen former residents said they were handcuffed, whipped with belts, had their mouths taped, and were punched for offenses like drinking from a spring or singing. A 2024 civil suit added restraint with zip ties and denial of menstrual products. One woman said she broke bones and was told to "walk it off."1, 3

Why they matter

Circle of Hope operated with no state license because Missouri exempted religious schools. Its exposure, alongside Agape's, led Missouri to pass its 2021 law requiring religious boarding schools to notify the state and run staff background checks.2

Connections

  • Circle of Hope staff: former director Jeffrey Ables, named in the 2024 civil suit for doing nothing after girls reported abuse.3
