Ken Huey founded Calo (Change Academy Lake of the Ozarks) in Missouri in 2006. It became the leading program marketing "reconditioning" of adopted teens with trauma and attachment diagnoses. Calo later became the platform for Embark Behavioral Health, and in 2026 the Associated Press documented runaways, assaults and weak oversight there. He now runs the HOPE Group (Havenwood Academy) in Utah.1, 2, 6

Roles and timeline

  • 1994 onward: working in behavioral health; he says he is adopted himself.1
  • 2002: PhD in marriage and family therapy, Purdue.2
  • 2002-2006: therapist, then business development director and clinical director in residential treatment. The network map lists West Ridge Academy (clinical director) and Provo Canyon School (business director).2
  • Nov. 2006: founded Calo in Lake Ozark, Missouri.2
  • 2008: Calo supported by Kimball DeLaMare's Proficio Management Group.3
  • 2015: sold his interest in Calo and semi-retired.1
  • ~2020: joined the HOPE Group (Havenwood Academy, Zion Hills Academy, Eagle's Rest Ranch) as executive director, later CEO.1
  • Undated: board member of the Attachment & Trauma Network.4

The adoptee model

Calo markets itself to adoptive parents whose children have trauma histories, often diagnosed with reactive attachment disorder. Huey's own adoption is central to the pitch. A 2014 Missouri blog quoted Calo's website describing its work as "reconditioning" adopted children to accept their new families.5

He also sat on the board of the Attachment & Trauma Network, an advocacy group for adoptive families that named him an "Angel" in 2016 - putting a program owner inside a group adoptive parents turn to for guidance.4

Documented concerns at Calo

  • 2009: a 16-year-old student disappeared from Calo, prompting a public search and a parent group.5
  • 2010: Huey sued the parents of a student who ran away, for breach of contract; the suit was later dropped.5
  • 2011-2014: the first abuse lawsuits against Calo were filed; several were dismissed or settled.5
  • 2026: an AP investigation found runaways, assaults and little oversight at Calo, which charges up to $20,000 a month and is now largely funded by insurance and Medicaid. Calo denies wrongdoing.6, 7
