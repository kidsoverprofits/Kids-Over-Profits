The modern industry inherited its core idea, that a child can be improved by taking them away from home and putting them under total control, from institutions that are centuries older. These three timelines follow that idea from the ancient world to the eve of the 20th century. Read them in order, or jump to the one you need.

  • Precursors in antiquity
    The Spartan agoge, Plato's communal child-rearing, Aristotle on state-run education, and the Roman father's power of life and death over his children.
  • Medieval child oblation and monastic schools
    Children given permanently to monasteries, the rule-bound life inside the cloister, and the penitential convents that became the first Magdalene homes.
  • Orphan care through the ages
    Poor relief, workhouses, foundling hospitals, houses of refuge, and the orphan trains: how poverty became a reason to institutionalize children.
