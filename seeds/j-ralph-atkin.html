The corporate web

The International Survivors Action Committee (ISAC), a survivor group, studied the corporations tied to WWASPS and to J. Ralph Atkin. In thirty years (1961 to 1991) it counted about 16 such corporations; between 1996 and 2003 it counted 213. Of 254 it examined, "75 corporations used addresses that do not exist, were incorrect, or were listed as 'unknown.'"2

ISAC described the method as changing corporate names slightly in spelling, dissolving one entity and reincorporating it under a near-identical name, so state corporate records would not attach a history to the new corporation. Its example: "Teen Help, Inc has been renamed many times to Teens in Crisis 2001, Teen Help LLC 1997, Teen Assist, Teen Watch USA." Browning Academy, it noted, "does not physically exist; its corporate documents list non-existent addresses or the office of J. Ralph Atkin."2
