Thomas E. Bratter, Ed.D., also known as Tom Bratter, founded and led John Dewey Academy (JDA) in Great Barrington, Massachusetts, from 1985 until his death in 2012. The archived JDA history describes the school, its location at Searles Castle and its use of Caring Confrontation Psychotherapy (CCP).1, 2 In Lon Woodbury's 2005 interview, Bratter says his JDA work began in 1985 and describes his own professional background.11

Background and work

In the 2005 interview, Bratter says he earned an Ed.D. at Columbia University Teachers College with a dissertation titled "Confrontation: A Group Psychotherapeutic Treatment Model for Alienated, Unmotivated, Adolescent Drug Abusers." He says he started and directed six community prevention and treatment programs in Westchester County, worked in the 1970s as a psychotherapist at Teen Centers and director of a methadone clinic, and was an adjunct professor at Union Institute and the College of New Rochelle. He also describes consulting and private psychotherapy work in Scarsdale.11 Confirm degree and appointments against university and employment records.

The archived JDA history links CCP to a Springer article. Bratter's interview describes his model as drawing on confrontational therapeutic communities; use the article and his cited dissertation to support claims about the model's formal origin and methods.1, 2, 11

Practices described in accounts of JDA

The archived JDA history and supplied survivor notes describe peer-led criticism, public disclosure of vulnerabilities, humiliation, symbolic punishments, late-night sessions, degrading signs, body-shaming and chores. These are reports about particular experiences, not evidence that every student experienced every practice.1 The Berkshire Eagle 2021 investigation mentioned in your notes is not linked in either repo; add the exact article citation for those attributed accounts.4

Your notes also describe "Closed House" periods, food restrictions, sleeping on marble floors and a four-level peer-governance system. The archived JDA page describes peer-voted levels and reported isolation/sleeping conditions; cite individual survivor accounts for the additional details and dates.1, 5

In the interview, Bratter said JDA students spent at least three hours a day studying and at least ten hours in groups, and claimed that students did not receive psychotropic medication and had strong college and graduate-school outcomes. These are Bratter's statements in a 2005 interview, not independently verified outcome data or clinical records.11

The archived JDA history describes the school's student population, approximate capacity and program structure. Your notes add small class sizes, 1993 and 2004 private-school accreditations and a Massachusetts licensing claim; the school and agency records needed to support those specifics are not in either repo.1, 6
