Research draft - not for publication. The primary organizational history below is a selective personal account by John L. Santa and Jan Moss Courtney. It documents her role in NATSAP's formation and standards work; it does not establish responsibility for conduct at individual member programs.

Jan Moss Courtney, also known as Jan Moss, was Executive Director of Spring Ridge Academy when its founder, Jeannie Courtney, asked her to represent the school at the January 1999 NATSAP organizing meeting in Albuquerque. The article recounts that John Reddan organized the meeting, attendees formed the association and elected its first Board, and Jan became its Secretary.1, 2

NATSAP founding and leadership

The history describes Courtney's work producing early meeting minutes, helping the first Board stay organized, supporting conferences and expanding regional chapters. It says she later served as NATSAP Executive Director and helped the organization support committees and broaden its activities. The supplied timeline gives her NATSAP Executive Director/President period as 2004-2009; cite the dated appointment and departure records for those exact endpoints.1, 2

The article says NATSAP prioritized ethical-practice standards and describes retreats, committee work and member consensus. It explicitly credits Jared Balmer with supplying a working draft as a framework for discussion, while describing Courtney's organizational and minute-taking contributions. This supports her role in the process, but not sole authorship of every standard.1 NATSAP membership and voluntary standards should not be described as accreditation or state licensing.3

2007 congressional hearing

At the October 10, 2007 House Committee on Education and Labor hearing, the witness list identifies Moss as NATSAP's Executive Director. She testified that NATSAP was a trade organization, did not certify programs or conduct investigations, and relied on state regulators, accreditors and other third parties when reviewing member concerns. Her prepared statement also described the association's ethics principles, complaint process, membership requirements and response to the GAO report.6

Members questioned Moss about deaths at member programs, self-certification, complaint counts, accreditation and the meaning of membership. In response, she said NATSAP had made mistakes and needed to take the concerns back to its Board; Chairman George Miller replied that he believed the association was missing a duty of care. These are statements in the hearing, not court findings about Moss or NATSAP.6
