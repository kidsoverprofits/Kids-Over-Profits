Steve Cartisano (~1956-2019) founded the Challenger Foundation in 1988, the forced-march wilderness program where 16-year-old Kristen Chase died of heatstroke in 1990. He was acquitted of negligent homicide and child abuse in 1992, then kept opening programs offshore - in Hawaii, Samoa and Canada - as each closed. He is the textbook case of a banned operator moving beyond U.S. regulators' reach, and the subject of Netflix's Hell Camp (2023).1, 2

Roles and timeline

  • 1988: founded the Challenger Foundation in Utah: 500-mile desert hikes for teens, with rapid national growth.1
  • June 27, 1990: Kristen Chase, 16, collapsed and died of exertional heatstroke on the Kaiparowits Plateau in 90-degree heat.2
  • Aug. 1990: charged with negligent homicide and child abuse, with field director Lance Jagger.3
  • May 28, 1992: a jury acquitted him on all six counts. He was barred from operating teen survival programs in Utah.2
  • 1994: Chase's parents settled their federal suit for $260,000.1
  • Early 1990s: founded Challenger Hawaii; the map also lists Challenger Utah.7
  • 1998-1999: hired by Utah businessmen to start New Hope Academy in Samoa, which closed in 1999 and reportedly stranded five youths.4
  • 1999: Pacific Coast Academy in Samoa; the U.S. embassy and Samoan officials removed the teens after abuse allegations.4, 5
  • Undated: co-founded Chilanko Lodge (British Columbia).7
  • May 4, 2019: died of a heart attack in Durant, Oklahoma, at 63.6

Methods

Challenger billed itself as turning "out of control" kids into leaders through survival hikes. Eight former participants said they were hit, starved and tied to trees. One described being hogtied and dragged over rocks after refusing to hike.1

At Pacific Coast Academy, a former student said she was hogtied and sexually assaulted by a village chief, and counselors ignored it. A secretly filmed video exposed conditions there.4

Pitching New Hope to investors, Cartisano reportedly promised the venture would turn a $25,000 start-up into $10.9 million.4

Influence

At least one former Challenger employee went on to run his own Utah program, where Michelle Sutton died in 1990. Her mother, Cathy Sutton, became a leading advocate for federal regulation.1
