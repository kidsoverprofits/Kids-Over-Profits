Lon Woodbury was admissions director at CEDU's Rocky Mountain Academy who became one of the industry's first independent educational consultants in 1989. For nearly three decades he published Places for Struggling Teens, the Woodbury Reports newsletter and strugglingteens.com - the trade's main news source and directory, which sold paid placements. He steered families to programs while publishing their press releases: the referral pipeline in person rather than an operator.1, 2

Roles and timeline

  • 1960s: public school teacher.1
  • Aug. 1984: hired as admissions coordinator, then admissions director, at Rocky Mountain Academy (Bonners Ferry, ID), CEDU's sister school.1
  • ~1989: left RMA after about five years; became an independent educational consultant and founded Places for Struggling Teens.1, 2
  • 1989-2017: publisher of Woodbury Reports and strugglingteens.com.1
  • 1996: formed a consulting partnership with former RMA staffer Linda Shaffer.3
  • 2005: quoted defending CEDU as it collapsed; told the Spokesman-Review RMA's reputation suffered for reasons other than lawsuits.4
  • 2018: published a memoir series on the CEDU/RMA experience, including interviews about Synanon's influence on CEDU.5

How the newsletter worked

Woodbury Reports mixed news ("Seen 'n Heard"), program visit reports and staff moves with paid advertising. Some profiles were labeled paid placements, including Kreg Gillman's 2004 interview as Provo Canyon CEO. Program leaders like Gillman, Ken Huey and Brad Gerrard visited his office. Its archive of staff moves and openings is now one of the best records of how people circulated between programs.1, 3

He described CEDU and RMA as a "grand experiment" drawing on 1960s approaches "not necessarily proven." In 2018 he framed RMA's founding in terms of A Nation at Risk and public school criticism.1, 5

Why he matters

Educational consultants send families to programs and are often the first professional a desperate parent trusts. Woodbury helped build that profession while running the industry's main trade publication, and he came out of CEDU, one of the most criticized lineages in this series. The newsletter did note some industry-wide problems, such as the Hartford Courant's 1998 series on restraint deaths.1

Connections

  • CEDU lineage: Mel Wasserman, Dan Earle (RMA founding headmaster), Linda Shaffer, Bill Lane.
  • Industry contacts: Kreg Gillman, Ken Huey, Brad Gerrard, and Kathy Nussberger (former Woodbury Reports editor).
  • Kids Over Profits: the site's Woodbury Facts pipeline draws on his archive.
