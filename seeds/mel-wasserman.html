Mel Wasserman founded CEDU in 1967 after joining Synanon; the archived CEDU history describes the program as based on Synanon's confrontational methods and connects him with Rocky Mountain Academy and later CEDU programs. It reports that Wasserman died in 2002.1, 2

In a 2005 interview, Tom Bratter recalled meeting Wasserman at an international therapeutic-communities conference in Bangkok in the mid-1970s. Bratter said they discussed whether and how those concepts could be applied to schools for troubled adolescents. This is a retrospective report of their conversation, not a complete or independently corroborated account of Wasserman's role.7

Reported practices in the intake notes

The archived CEDU history describes confrontational "rap" sessions and reports of hard labor and other strict program practices. Your notes add sleep deprivation and wilderness survival programs. These reports apply to specific schools and periods, not necessarily every CEDU program.1, 2

Accountability and boundaries

Larry Stednitz's January 2006 Woodbury Reports essay describes CEDU as having operated eight programs and served more than 500 young people daily at its peak. Stednitz characterizes Wasserman as a non-clinician and says he was not supportive of clinicians; treat both as the essay author's account, not independently audited facts.8

Stednitz says the essay draws primarily on a staff training Wasserman conducted at Rocky Mountain Academy in December 1990. As reported there, Wasserman treated accountability and clear, rigid boundaries as central to the CEDU model, described enrollment as a privilege tied to following CEDU's values, and argued that staff should set limits without apology. The essay quotes his maxim "values not bars" and his view that boundaries should be firm and non-negotiable. These are attributed descriptions of a training, not a direct copy of the training document.8

The interview with Tom Bratter separately reports that he met Wasserman at a therapeutic-communities conference in Bangkok in the mid-1970s and discussed applying these concepts to schools for troubled adolescents.7
