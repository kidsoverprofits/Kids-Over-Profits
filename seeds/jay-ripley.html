Research draft - not for publication. Harms below are attributed to programs, not to Ripley personally, unless a source ties him to them directly. Check every item against its source before publishing.

John "Jay" Ripley co-founded Sequel Youth and Family Services in 1999 and led it through its growth to more than 30 facilities. In 2021, as Sequel was collapsing under abuse reports, he founded Vivant Behavioral Healthcare, which bought most of Sequel's remaining programs.1, 2, 3

Roles and timeline

  • 1979-1990: accountant at Ernst & Young (1979-1985), then controller and vice president at Jiffy Lube (1985-1990).6
  • 1990-1995: a founding stockholder of Youth Services International (YSI), started with his mentor, Jiffy Lube founder James Hindman, and its president, chief operating officer and chief financial officer.1, 6
  • 1995-1998: chief executive of Precision Auto Care.6
  • 1999: co-founded Sequel with Adam Shapiro and Cindy Cox, when the board of the nonprofit that owned Clarinda Academy in Iowa, a former YSI program, asked them to take it over.2, 6
  • 2009: Sequel acquired Three Springs, Inc., including Auldern Academy and five Alabama facilities.
  • 2011: took the chief executive's role again after the Camelot System of Care deal backed by Levine Leichtman Capital Partners, as Shapiro stepped back; John Stupak followed him as chief executive in 2013.7, 6
  • 2014: became Sequel's majority owner, according to the 2023 Bates complaint.8
  • 2017: Sequel's proposed merger into a public blank-check company disclosed a founder fee of $104,167 a month paid to him and 3,140,739 shares for his revocable trust; Sequel ended the deal in May.6
  • 2017: stayed on as chairman when private equity firm Altamont Capital bought a majority stake.
  • 2021: founded Vivant Behavioral Healthcare, which bought 13 Sequel facilities. In a 2022 federal filing Sequel's lawyers said Vivant's parent, Vivant Behavioral Holdings, is owned by Ripley Holdings, LLC (held by Ripley and a family trust), his daughter Christine Aron and four other people.3, 9
  • 2022: Vivant received one of four letters from Senators Wyden and Murray opening the Senate Finance Committee investigation. The committee's 2024 report said Ripley "has established, sold, and reacquired many RTFs, making it difficult to keep track of him or hold him accountable."4, 3
  • 2023-2025: named personally as a defendant in Bates v. Sequel, a class action alleging forced unpaid labor by children in Sequel programs (the court kept him in the case in October 2025), and in the 2025 suit by 13 former residents of Mingus Mountain Academy alleging sexual abuse.8, 10

Business model

Ripley explained his model at a 2015 University of Baltimore lecture: he described making good margins on the daily rate agencies pay per child, and said the money comes from controlling staffing. The Senate Finance Committee's 2024 report used that lecture as an example of perverse incentives.3

Sequel relied on public money from more than 40 states. APM Reports found that many of the nonprofit boards overseeing Sequel facilities were filled with people tied to Ripley and former YSI executives, including a banker Ripley personally recruited.5

Documented harms under his companies

These events occurred at facilities his companies owned or operated; none of the cited reports is quoted here as tying Ripley personally to an incident.

  • 1995, YSI: Mystie Kreimeyer, 15, died of a pulmonary embolism after days of untreated symptoms.
  • 2015-2021, Clarinda Academy: restraint injuries, a counselor's sexual abuse conviction, and removal of all Washington and Nebraska youth. Sequel closed it in February 2021.2
  • 2017, Sequel TSI (Madison, AL): two residents who escaped allegedly killed Van Johnson, 61. Madison revoked the facility's license in 2019.
  • 2020, Lakeside Academy (MI): Cornelius Frederick, 16, died after a restraint. Two staff later pleaded no contest to manslaughter.
  • 2021-2022: Mountain Home Academy, Pompano Youth Treatment, Forest Ridge and Charles Britt Academy closed after abuse findings.

After the sale, Vivant used the change of ownership to fight lawsuits against former Sequel facilities.1
